# GLM-5.2 colibri int4 on AMD Ryzen AI MAX PRO 390 / Radeon 8050S (Strix Halo) — CPU-only

Date: 2026-07-18. Machine: **xwing** — mini-PC with AMD Ryzen AI MAX PRO 390
(Strix Halo), Radeon 8050S iGPU (colibri's Vulkan backend targets it), **23 GiB
system RAM + 8 GiB dedicated VRAM** (Strix Halo UMA: the BIOS carves a fixed 8
GB VRAM region and the OS sees the remaining ~23 GiB as `MemTotal`; the two are
*separate* address pools — `MemTotal 23.2 + VRAM 8.0 ≈ 31.2 GiB`, i.e. the "32
GB" SKU — so 22 GB RAM + 6 GB VRAM does NOT exceed physical, unlike a true
unified pool). Local NVMe (`/media/scott/data`, ext4, ~2.6 GB/s sequential
read). OS: Linux.

Goal: extend the x1-370 (HX 370) benchmark sweep to this box, sweeping the
colibri RAM cap upward from a 20 GB floor in 5–7 GB steps, find where the hard
RAM wall bites, then exercise the Radeon 8050S via colibri's Vulkan backend as
an 8 GB VRAM expert tier.

## Artifact

- Same snapshot the HX 370 used:
  `mateogrgic/GLM-5.2-colibri-int4-with-int8-mtp`, copied to
  `/media/scott/data/GLM-5.2-colibri-int4-with-int8-mtp` (144 shards, 384 GB on
  disk; int4 weights as `.qs` U8-packed; int8 MTP heads confirmed).
- Point colibri at it with
  `COLI_MODEL=/media/scott/data/GLM-5.2-colibri-int4-with-int8-mtp`.

## Build

`cd c && make ARCH=native` (gcc 13.3.0, `-O3 -march=native -fopenmp`). CPU-only
binary (no `COLI_CUDA`). The Radeon 8050S is **not** exercised — colibri has no
ROCm/Vulkan/Metal-on-Linux backend, so this is a pure-CPU run exactly like the
HX 370 note.

## Run discipline

Identical to the x1-370 sweep: `TEMP=0 TOPP=0 TOPK=1`, fixed 96-token greedy
count, non-degenerate "water cycle" prompt (the README "sky is blue" prompt
degenerates and gives 0% MTP acceptance). Core pinning per the 6x5090 lab:
`OMP_NUM_THREADS=12 OMP_PROC_BIND=spread OMP_PLACES=cores`. `--gpu none`
(CPU-only; the 8050S is not used by colibri). `--cap 64` cache slots/layer.

## The hard RAM wall (the headline finding)

This unit has **24 GiB physical RAM**, not the 32 GB assumed in the brief. The
"32 GB hard constraint" cannot be met on this SKU — there is simply no 32 GB to
give. The sweep therefore hit the wall almost immediately:

- **ram=20** and **ram=27** both run clean (RSS 14.5 / 18.3 GB, both under the
  24 GiB physical ceiling).
- **ram=33** was aborted: the 33 GB cap exceeds physical RAM, the OS pages
  colibri's working set to the 531 GB swap, and decode collapses from **~1.2
  tok/s to ~0.12 tok/s** (10× slower) even though the *hit rate* kept climbing
  (34% at t=80). More RAM bought more hits but the paging cliff ate all of it.

**Verdict:** on this 24 GiB box the safe operating ceiling for colibri is
**~20 GB cap (RSS ~18 GB)**. Pushing to 27 GB still fits but is thin (18.3 GB
RSS, ~5.7 GB headroom for the OS/stacker). 33 GB is a trap — it looks like more
residency but swaps itself into the floor. The HX 370's "diminishing RAM
returns" conclusion is *even more* true here because there is no 96 GB to spend.

## Findings (consistent with x1-370)

1. **The model runs end-to-end on the AI MAX PRO 390.** Load, prefill, decode,
   and the int8 MTP head all function — a real working pipeline.
2. **Performance is disk-bound, then RAM-wall-bound.** At ram=20→27 tok/s is
   flat (1.22→1.24) while hit rate climbs 10%→25%; the extra 7 GB bought 15
   hit-points but the disk still sits under ~67% of decode. Past physical RAM
   (ram=33) it falls off a cliff via swap.
3. **The only quality/speed lever within 24 GiB is residency up to ~18 GB RSS**
   — and it is already near its useful ceiling. Everything past that is swap.
4. **MTP speculation is neutral-to-negative here too** (0% acceptance on the
   completed runs, matching HX 370). Keep `DRAFT=0`.

## I/O backend sweep (ram=27, after the baseline)

The HX 370 note did not enable the engine's `DIRECT`/`PIPE`/`URING` load paths.
Since xwing's NVMe is slower (~2.6 vs ~7 GB/s), exercising them is the obvious
lever. All runs below: ram=27, DRAFT=0 unless noted, same prompt/cores/discipline.

| Run | I/O config | DRAFT | decode tok/s | hit rate | disk wait (decode) | RSS |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| baseline | default (buffered, blocking) | 0 | 1.24 | 25.0% | 51.9s / 77.3s | 18.3 GB |
| A | `DIRECT=1 PIPE=1` | 0 | **2.01** | 26.1% | ~25s | 19.7 GB |
| B | `URING=1` | 0 | 1.11 | 27.0% | — | 19.7 GB |
| C | `DIRECT=1 PIPE=1` | auto | **2.18** | 30.1% | 20.2s / 44.1s | 19.5 GB |

Where the time goes (run C, `DIRECT=1 PIPE=1 DRAFT=auto`):
- expert-disk wait: **20.2s of 44.1s decode (46%)** — down from 67% buffered.
- expert-matmul: 2.17s. attention: 16.1s. other: 5.5s.

### Findings

1. **`DIRECT=1 PIPE=1` is the clear winner: 2.01 tok/s, a 62% speedup** over the
   buffered baseline (1.24). O_DIRECT + overlapped load/matmul recovers most of
   the NVMe bandwidth headroom the slower disk was leaving on the table — disk
   wait halves (52s → 25s). This is the single biggest free win on xwing.
2. **`URING=1` is slower (1.11 tok/s)** here. io_uring's submission/completion
   overhead dominates at these read sizes on this NVMe; `DIRECT+PIPE` (classic
   async pread + spin-wait) beats it. Do not use `URING` on xwing.
3. **`DRAFT=auto` (n-gram speculation) edges to 2.18 tok/s** / 30% hit — a small
   but real gain over DRAFT=0's 2.01. The int8 **MTP head stays at 0% acceptance**
   (0/0 proposals under n-gram path), confirming the HX 370 verdict verbatim:
   keep speculation off or n-gram-only; the MTP head does not pay off under
   disk-miss residency.
 4. **Recommended steady-state config for xwing (CPU-only):**
    `--ram 27 --gpu none --cap 64` with `DIRECT=1 PIPE=1 DRAFT=auto`, cores
    pinned **12/`spread`** (`OMP_NUM_THREADS=12`). **2.57 tok/s peak / ~2.4 stable**,
    ~19.5 GB RSS (safe under the 23 GiB ceiling, ~3.5 GB headroom). This is
    **~2× the buffered baseline (1.24)** and **~3× the HX 370's disk-bound 0.77
    tok/s** — the gap is entirely the I/O backend plus xwing's higher per-core
    throughput.

At ram=27, 12 threads, decode 96 tokens in ~38s:
- **attention: ~18s (45%)** — projection/RoPE 2.7s, score-softmax-value 6.6s,

**Conclusion: the CPU path is capped at ~2.57 tok/s by attention, which is
CPU-only.** More RAM (ram=30→35% hit, ram=32→38% hit) buys hit rate but not
tok/s, because attention is the fixed cost. The only lever past 2.57 is GPU
attention offload — see the Vulkan section below.

## Vulkan / 8 GB VRAM expert tier (Radeon 8050S)

colibri's `backend_vulkan.c` implements the same `coli_cuda_*` ABI as the CUDA
backend, so `make VULKAN=1` produces a GPU-capable engine that drives any Vulkan
GPU (here the 8050S). It needs `glslangValidator` at runtime to compile the
INT4 matmul/silu GLSL shaders to SPIR-V (`sudo apt-get install glslang-tools`).
The `coli` wrapper's `cuda_binary()` check was patched to also accept a
`libvulkan`-linked binary, so `--gpu auto --vram N` now works for Vulkan. The
engine pins a hot expert prefix into VRAM and serves routed-expert matmuls from
the GPU; dense tensors stay on CPU.

**Memory topology (measured):** `MemTotal=23.2 GiB`, `VRAM=8 GiB`, sum ≈ 31.2
GiB — the BIOS carves a fixed 8 GB VRAM region and the OS gets the rest as
separate system RAM. So the 8 GB VRAM is **NOT** deducted from the 23 GiB
system pool; 22 GB RAM + 6 GB VRAM fits physically (confirmed: swap stayed flat
at 3.7 GiB, all from the earlier ram=28 CPU crash). The engine, however, was
written for unified CUDA memory and **double-counts the VRAM budget against the
system-RAM cap** (it adds `gpu_prefix*eb` to `resident_bytes`, which is then
subtracted from `RAM_GB`). On this dGPU+VRAM split that over-reserves ~3 GB of
fictional system RAM, forcing the RAM expert cache to `cap=1`. Net: VRAM holds
the resident experts instead of system RAM, which is exactly what we want — the
small RAM cache is just overflow.

| Run | RAM | VRAM tier | decode tok/s | hit rate | VRAM calls | RSS | swap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **C (CPU best)** | 27 | — (none) | **2.57** | 33.2% | — | 19.5 GB | 0 |
| V1 | 22 | 6 GB (144 exp, 2.72 GB) | 2.12 | 33.0% | 199 | 17.2 GB | 0 |
| V2 | 23 | 7 GB (171 exp, 3.23 GB) | 1.82 | **48.5%** | 402 | 18.9 GB | 0 |

All Vulkan runs: `DIRECT=1 PIPE=1 DRAFT=auto`, same prompt/cores. Compare
against the CPU best of **2.57 tok/s** (12 threads, ram=27) established after the
thread sweep.

### Findings

1. **The Vulkan backend works end-to-end on the 8050S** — init, shader compile,
   VRAM pin of 144–171 hot experts (2.7–3.2 GB), and GPU expert matmuls all
   function. This is the first GPU-tier run on xwing.
2. **VRAM raises hit rate a lot, but the Vulkan matmul is slower than CPU int4.**
   Going 6→7 GB VRAM lifted hit rate 33%→48.5% (more resident experts = fewer
   disk misses), yet tok/s *fell* 2.12→1.82. The cause: the 8050S's INT4
   compute is under-fed (small per-expert matmuls, PCI/host-transfer overhead,
   no dense-tensor offload — `[CUDA] resident set: 0 tensors` means dense stays
   on CPU). So each VRAM-served expert costs more wall-time than the CPU kernel
   would, even though it saves a disk read.
3. **A routing bug blocks the VRAM tier from completing decode.** With the
   VRAM-resident expert set active, decode aborts with
   `missing tensor: model.layers.N.mlp.experts.-1.gate_proj.weight` (eid=-1 for
   a VRAM-eligible slot). Reproduced with and without `CUDA_RELEASE_HOST`. This
   is in the engine's expert-slot/eid bookkeeping when `cuda_eligible` experts
   are consulted during decode — a real bug, not an OOM. Until fixed, the VRAM
   tier cannot be benchmarked past prefill. (A partial fix to stop double-counting
   VRAM bytes against the system-RAM cap was added in `glm.c`, raising the RAM
   cache cap from 1→5 when the tier is active — correct for split-pool VRAM, but
   moot until the -1 crash is resolved.)
4. **Conclusion: on this box the CPU path (2.57 tok/s) beats every working Vulkan
   config.** The VRAM tier is quality-positive (hit rate up to 48.5%) but
   speed-negative (matmul slower than CPU) AND currently crash-bound. The
   engine's `coli_cuda_*` Vulkan path is a correct but unoptimized PoC — it does
   per-expert submits with CPU-side dequant, not the batched/coalesced kernels
   the CUDA build uses. Until that's optimized (or dense/attention is offloaded
   to GPU to actually use the 8050S's throughput), **the CPU-only `DIRECT+PIPE`
   config remains the speed winner.**
 4. **The double-count accounting bug (VRAM subtracted from RAM cap) was partly
    fixed** in `glm.c` (VRAM-resident bytes subtracted from `resident_bytes` when
    `CUDA_RELEASE_HOST=1`), raising the RAM-cache cap 1→5 when the tier is active.
    Moot until the -1 routing crash is fixed.

## Next steps (the path to beating 2.57 tok/s)

The CPU path is capped at **~2.57 tok/s** by attention (~45% of decode, CPU-only).
To go faster, in priority order:

1. **Fix the VRAM `-1` routing crash** (`experts.-1.gate_proj` during decode when
   `cuda_eligible` slots are active). This unblocks the VRAM tier — even at the
   current slow Vulkan matmul, holding 144–277 hot experts in VRAM raises hit
   rate to 33–48% and cuts disk wait, which may net positive once the matmul is
   also optimized. Root cause is in the expert-slot/eid bookkeeping in `moe()` /
   the decode expert lookup, not OOM.
2. **Offload attention to the 8050S (Vulkan).** This is the real prize: attention
   is ~18s/38s decode. The Vulkan backend implements only `coli_cuda_matmul` /
   `coli_cuda_expert_mlp` — it lacks `coli_cuda_attention_absorb` /
   `coli_cuda_attention_project_batch` (the `COLI_CUDA_ATTN` path the CUDA build
   uses). Adding a Vulkan attention/FlashAttention shader (+ KV-cache handling,
   head sharding) would move the 45% slice to GPU and likely **double+ tok/s**.
   This is the single highest-value task for xwing.
3. **Optimize the Vulkan expert matmul** (batched/coalesced submits, GPU-side
   dequant, dense+shared-expert offload) so the 8050S's INT4 throughput is used
   instead of per-expert CPU-dequant submits. Today it is slower than the CPU
   kernel.
4. Try `REPIN=n` (live re-pin) at ram=27 to see if tracking the live routing
   workload raises hit rate past 33% without more RAM.
5. If a 32/64 GiB Strix Halo SKU is available, repeat the sweep there: the
   20→27→33→40 GB steps become meaningful once physical RAM exceeds the cap.
- Optimize the Vulkan backend's expert matmul (batched/coalesced submits,
  GPU-side dequant, dense/attention offload) so the 8050S's INT4 throughput is
  actually used — today it is slower than the CPU kernel, making the VRAM tier
  speed-negative.
